## 1. SPACs, 2020–21 cohort

**Klausner, Ohlrogge & Ruan** is *"A Sober Look at SPACs"*, Yale J. on Reg. 39(1): 228–303 (2022); SSRN 3720919 (SSRN returned 403, so numbers come from the journal abstract and the authors' HLS Forum post of 19 Nov 2020). Published abstract: "SPACs raise $10.00 per share … For SPACs that merged during our primary sample period of January 2019 through June 2020, mean and median net cash per share were $4.10 and $5.70" and June 2020–Nov 2021 mergers remained "far below $10". The HLS (working-paper) version used 47 SPACs (Jan 2019–Jun 2020), median cash per share $6.67, mean/median redemptions 58%/73%, promote "25% of the SPAC's IPO proceeds, or equivalently, 20% of post-IPO equity", median dilution "50.4% of cash delivered", and Table 3 three-month median return −14.5% with six- and twelve-month returns "worse still" — exact 6/12-month figures **unavailable** (full PDF behind SSRN/Yale DSpace). **Conflict to note:** the $6.67 (WP) vs $5.70 (published) medians are different definitions/samples; cite one consistently.

**SEC DERA staff report on SPACs: none found.** The sec.gov DERA staff-papers listing has no SPAC paper; what exists is the SEC's own economic analysis in Proposing Release 33‑11048 (30 Mar 2022, p.14: "A number of studies have found that returns are relatively poor for investors in companies following a de-SPAC transaction", fn 30 citing Klausner et al., Rodrigues & Stegemoller, Gahng‑Ritter‑Zhang) and Final Rule 33‑11265 (24 Jan 2024). Treat "SEC DERA report" as a non-source.

**Gahng, Ritter & Zhang, "SPACs", RFS 36(9) 2023** (Feb‑23‑2023 version): 152 de‑SPACs Jan 2010–Dec 2020, EW 1‑yr BHR **−11.3%** vs CRSP VW 19.4% (mkt‑adj −30.7%); public‑cash‑weighted −3.0%; medians −26.1% (1‑yr) and −39.4% (3‑yr); 3‑yr EW −12.0% vs 42.6%; fn 21: 146 de‑SPACs in Q1–Q3 2021 had EW 1‑yr −62.1%, pulling the pooled EW 1‑yr to −36.2%. **2020–21 cohort (Ritter, IPOs‑SPACs.pdf, 10 Jul 2026, Table 15c updated 31 Dec 2025, EW means — medians are not published):** 2020 mergers n=64: 1‑yr −3.0% (mkt‑adj −35.6%), 3‑yr −56.0% (mkt‑adj −84.6%); 2021 mergers n=198: 1‑yr **−64.2%** (mkt‑adj −53.9%), 3‑yr **−73.0%** (mkt‑adj −80.0%); 2022 n=102: 1‑yr −63.8%; 2012–2022 pooled n=451: 1‑yr −46.3%, 3‑yr −57.7%. Renaissance (2022 Annual Review): "2021's class of VC deals now average a return of −58%". No source reports a *median* 12/24‑month de‑SPAC return for the 2020–21 cohort; use Ritter's EW means and say so.

## 2. Odlyzko; railway mania

**(a) Lit‑fibre share — not in Odlyzko.** Full texts of *The Many Paradoxes of Broadband* (rev. 15 Jul 2003; First Monday 8(9)) and *Internet traffic growth: Sources and implications* (2003) contain no statement of what fraction of long‑haul fibre was lit (searched "lit", "dark fiber", "TeleGeography", "Merrill"). What he does say: "the fiber glut is here … using ('lighting') it is getting less expensive all the time" (Paradoxes p.18); access‑link "utilizations are typically in the 3–5% range", backbone links "around 10%", 1998 backbones "between 10 and 15% of their capacity" (Traffic growth p.8). **Conflict:** the referee's 2.5–5% and the secondary ~10% are utilisation figures, not lit‑share, and should not be attributed to Odlyzko; the lit‑share figure remains **unavailable** (TeleGeography/Merrill primary not retrieved).

**(b) Railway mania.** Odlyzko, *Collective hallucinations…* (prelim. 15 Jan 2010; SSRN 1537338): railway investment £44m in 1847 = **7.3% of GDP** (£600m) (p.34); Table 1 gives £13.1m/30.0m/43.9m/33.1m for 1845–48; "By the end of 1850, British investors had put about £250 million into their country's railways, almost half of their GDP" (p.23); miles sanctioned 1843 91 → 1844 805 → 1845 2,700 → **1846 4,538** → 1847 1,354 → 1850 8 (p.8); share index peak Jul 1845 167.9 → trough Oct 1849 60.5 (p.78) = −64%. *This time is different* (rev. 21 Jun 2010): 1830s mania "over 8% of their country's GDP" (p.2); *The collapse of the Railway Mania…* (2011, p.3): 1847 peak "almost 8% of British GDP, and twice the military budget". A single authorised‑vs‑built miles figure is not stated. **Campbell & Turner, BHR 86(1) 2012** (accepted MS p.5): index peaked **1,984 on 8 Aug 1845**, 1,623 by end‑Nov 1845, low **673 on 19 Apr 1850** (−66%). **Campbell, EEH 49(1) 2012** (MPRA 61115): established‑railway index 1,000 (Jan 1843) → 1,717 (8 Aug 1845), −18.4% by end‑Nov 1845, trough Apr 1850. The "2,062 → 741, −64.1%" figures seen in a search snippet were not located in the texts checked (SINGLE‑SOURCE).

## 3. FCIC; Enron; Lehman

**FCIC Final Report (Jan 2011), OCR of GPO PDF:** p.xxv "83% of the mortgage securities rated triple‑A that year [2006] ultimately were downgraded"; p.222 "Moody's would downgrade 83% of all the 2006 Aaa mortgage‑backed securities tranches and all of the Baa tranches. For those securities issued in the second half of 2007, nearly all Aaa and Baa tranches were downgraded"; pp.222–23 of all investment‑grade tranches "76% of those issued in 2006 were downgraded to junk, as were 89% of those from 2007"; p.224 CDOs: "more than 90% of all tranches of CDOs had been downgraded … more than 80% of Aaa CDO bonds and more than 90% of Baa CDO bonds were eventually downgraded to junk"; pp.228–29: for 2005–07 triple‑A RMBS "only about 10% of Alt‑A and 4% of subprime securities had been 'materially impaired'". S&P/Moody's transition studies themselves: **unavailable** (not retrieved).

**Enron:** Senate hearing *Rating the Raters* (S. Hrg. 107‑471, 20 Mar 2002), S&P's Ronald Barone: "From December 1995 until November 1, 2001, Standard & Poor's rating of Enron was BBB‑plus"; "On November 28 … Standard & Poor's lowered Enron's rating to B‑minus, a non‑investment grade rating." Senate staff report *Enron's Credit Rating* (S. Prt. 107‑83, 3 Jan 2003): collapse 2 Dec 2001 (p.1); Moody's to Baa3 on 9 Nov 2001 (p.7); below investment grade 28 Nov 2001 (p.13). **Confirmed**; note S&P went BBB+ → (Nov 1) BBB → (Nov 9) BBB− → (Nov 28) B−, i.e. the "BBB+ → junk" framing skips two steps.

**Lehman:** 424B2 pricing supplement dated 31 Jul 2008 (filed 4 Aug): "rated A2 by Moody's, A by Standard & Poor's and A+ by Fitch." Moody's release 10 Sep 2008: LBHI "senior at A2" placed on review with direction uncertain; Fed market call 11 Sep 2008 (FCIC Lehman chronology, FRASER Tab 40): Moody's said the plan was "insufficient for them to maintain their current 'A2' credit rating." Filing 15 Sep 2008. Moody's A2 on 12 Sep is primary‑verified; S&P "A" on 12 Sep is consistent with the July filing but no S&P document dated 9–14 Sep was retrieved (SECONDARY).

## 4. Lock‑up expiry

Field & Hanka, JF 56(2) 2001 (1,948 lockups): "a permanent 40 percent increase in average trading volume, and a statistically prominent three‑day abnormal return of −1.5 percent", larger for VC‑backed (abstract; SECONDARY relay). Bradley, Jordan, Roten & Yi, JFR 24(4) 2001 (2,529 firms 1988–97): "significant and negative abnormal returns … concentrated in firms with venture capital backing" — numeric CARs **unavailable** (SSRN/Wiley blocked). Brav & Gompers, RFS 16(1) 2003 (2,794 IPOs): published abstract gives no expiration return; WP version (SSRN 204094) "average abnormal return at lock‑up expiration is −1.2%" (SECONDARY). Ofek & Richardson (2000, NYU Stern WP): ~40% volume rise, downward‑sloping demand; return magnitude **not verified** (SINGLE‑SOURCE).

## 5. Minsky; Kindleberger–Aliber; Perez

Minsky, *The Financial Instability Hypothesis*, Levy Economics Institute Working Paper No. 74, May 1992 (Levy page: 10 May 1992), written for the *Handbook of Radical Political Economy* (Elgar 1993) — **confirmed**; PDF at levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/wp74.pdf. *Manias, Panics, and Crashes*: 7th ed. 2015 (Aliber & Kindleberger, Palgrave), **8th ed. 2023 (Aliber, Kindleberger & McCauley, Palgrave Macmillan, ISBN 9783031160073)** with new crypto and US‑as‑lender‑of‑last‑resort chapters (SECONDARY listings). Stage names: book text not accessible (Springer JS‑gated; archive.org restricted); secondary summaries give displacement → boom/credit expansion → euphoria (overtrading) → distress → revulsion/panic — treat exact wording as **unverified**. Perez: phase names Irruption/Frenzy – Turning Point – Synergy/Maturity are from the 2002 book (not accessed); her June 2025 SPRU slides show "INSTALLATION PERIOD / 'TURNING POINT' / DEPLOYMENT PERIOD" and "Bubble 'prosperity' – Turning point – 'Golden Age' prosperity". **Perez on AI:** *"What Is AI's Place in History?"*, Project Syndicate, 11 Mar 2024: AI "is better understood as a key development within the still‑unfolding ICT revolution"; "a revolutionary technology is not the same thing as a technological revolution"; *Development and Change* interview (Lebdioui, Oct 2025): ICT revolution "including artificial intelligence (AI) as the latest addition"; Luohan Academy lecture 9 Dec 2025. **Conflict:** no Perez statement found calling AI a bubble/frenzy or dating a turning point to 2026–27 — those are third‑party blog glosses.
